# Authorship and specificity

When an existing interface or proposed repair feels generated, cheap, interchangeable, or over-templated, or the surface is visually busy without being designed. Causality test, not a blacklist of styles.

## Diagnose the real failure

Usually lacks a product-caused decision:

- same centered or stacked skeleton survives every noun and logo change
- cards, chips, badges, gradients, blur, giant type, or terminal chrome appear because the category suggested them, not because the task requires them
- fake charts, metrics, testimonials, logos, dashboards, terminals, or screenshots counterfeit proof
- dead controls imply functionality the artifact does not have
- every section repeats the same container, spacing, hierarchy, and reveal
- component polish is high while the primary artifact remains subordinate
- motion decorates arrival but explains no state, relationship, or continuity
- a signature move can be removed without changing comprehension, workflow, evidence, or character

Do not “fix” this by auto-selecting monochrome brutalism, beige luxury, acid grids, oversized serif italics, chrome, or hostile motion. A predictable rebellion is still predictable.

## Run the causality test

For each major decision:

~~~text
decision:
caused by: product | data | workflow | audience | material | place | proof | interaction
visible consequence:
what a competitor could not reuse:
state where it earns its cost:
kill condition:
~~~

Cause is only a style adjective → costume. A competitor can transplant the whole surface by replacing nouns → not authored yet.

At least one major decision must come from the actual artifact, workflow, data shape, or proof object. Brand personality can sharpen that decision; it must not replace it.

## Require real specificity

Use: real product objects and domain language; plausible data shapes with explicit fixture provenance; verified screenshots, media, outputs, places, or materials; real action verbs and consequences; constraints of frequency, pressure, input, aspect ratio, and state; existing brand or product assets when they earned continuity.

If evidence is missing, show the gap. Do not invent customers, awards, metrics, terminal output, maps, charts, or product behavior to look complete.

If the decision depends on shape rather than truth, an illustrative mock can use clearly labeled synthetic data. Mark it synthetic. Distribution realistic enough to stress the layout. Never present it as product proof.

## Author a component ecology

Avoid the page-builder reflex: everything the same rounded card. Small ecology by role: frame or field; primary artifact; navigation or mode switch; control cluster; evidence or detail region; status and feedback; interruption or decision point.

Components can share tokens without identical containment. Borders, rules, spacing, background changes, typography, position, or direct adjacency when a card adds no meaning.

Repeated components need coherent geometry and state behavior. Variety is not permission for random radii, shadows, icon families, paddings, or motion. Authored system, not cloned composition.

## Spend detail where it changes belief

High-value craft: product-specific information shape; treated screenshots or artifacts; coherent icons and vectors at actual sizes; typography and line breaks; responsive crops and focal points; complete pending, error, permission, empty, and recovery states; motion tied to continuity or feedback; edge, alignment, scrollbar, and safe-area finish.

Low-value theater: decorative dashboard div art; impossible glass over unreadable content; generic particles, noise, grid overlays, or glowing orbs; random 3D tilt and cursor followers; animated counters without trusted values; fake browser chrome or terminal text; dozens of hover effects on controls used every minute.

Detail earns its cost if it improves task speed, trust, comprehension, orientation, or memorable product character.

## Non-website surfaces

Do not force complex interfaces into landing-page grammar.

### HUD or game interface

Play field, mechanic, glance distance, threat, safe area, and input own the composition — not nav bars, stacked sections, or marketing-card rhythm.

### Command center

Urgency, freshness, ownership, evidence, and next action own the hierarchy. A wallboard and an operator console are different modes, not responsive variants of a dashboard.

### Studio or editor

The artifact and direct manipulation own the frame. Toolbars and inspectors are supporting instruments, not feature panels.

### Spatial, immersive, or installation UI

Viewing distance, environment, latency, orientation, occlusion, hardware input, and recovery define the system. A 1440-pixel browser mock does not prove it.

### Dense data workspace

Comparison, selection, provenance, confidence, and decision consequences own the structure. Decorative whitespace cannot replace scan clarity.

## Cheap-generation gate

Before proof: real constraint that caused the composition; primary artifact and whether it visibly dominates; category default and second reflex killed; common pattern that stayed and why it is the clearest grammar; content/evidence real vs synthetic vs missing vs blocked; which controls are interactive; signature move and the task or meaning it serves; whether rhythm expresses hierarchy or repeats one gap; whether motion explains state/continuity or merely announces rendering; whether the surface survives long content, error, narrow width, and repeated use; whether a competitor can reuse it after a noun swap.

Defense is a list of fonts, colors, shadows, gradients, effects, or anti-pattern bans → fail. Pass when product causality remains visible without the process explanation.

## Replacement language

Do not say “make it less generic.” Say:

~~~text
generic structure:
why it could belong to anyone:
real product cause:
replacement composition:
content/assets required:
signature behavior:
states that must survive:
proof target:
~~~

Specificity is an implementation contract, not a compliment.
